destroyed the same session. Protocol: `methodology/bc039-protocol-v0.1.0.md` v0.1.0, frozen

(8 vCPU / 16 GB) and the cheapest available match at €0.063/h against cx43's €0.031/h.

## Headline: a null result on the primary outcome

Both tools captured everything. Reported as plainly as a difference would be, per the
addendum.

| signal | issued/arm | Langfuse | Phoenix |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLM spans | 200 | 200/200 | 200/200 |
| tool spans | 140 | 140/140 | 140/140 |
| retrieval spans | 60 | 60/60 | 60/60 |
| **all spans** | **400** | **400/400** | **400/400** |
| parent-child edges | 180 | 180/180 | 180/180 |
| error records | 40 | 40/40 | 40/40 |

Zero missing spans, zero mis-parented spans, zero lost error records, zero duplicates, in
either arm.

**What a 400/400 sample does and does not prove.** Wilson 95% lower bound on all-span
capture is **0.9905**. The honest statement is "no drop observed, and the data is consistent
with a true capture rate as low as 99.0%" — not "perfect". At the per-signal level the bounds
are weaker still: retrieval, with only 60 opportunities per arm, bottoms out at 0.9398.

## Secondary: no measurable overhead difference

Bootstrap percentile CIs (10,000 resamples, seed 20260812) on mean wall-time difference
against the mandatory uninstrumented control arm:

| arm | observed diff | 95% CI | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Langfuse | −0.254 s | [−1.147, +0.762] | not significant |
| Phoenix | +1.433 s | [−0.084, +3.148] | not significant |

Both intervals cross zero, so neither is reported as a winner or a loser.

**Langfuse measured *faster* than the uninstrumented control.** That is not a finding about
Langfuse; it is the clearest available evidence that at n=20 this design is dominated by
OpenAI API latency, not by instrumentation cost. Medians: control 5.657 s, Langfuse 5.600 s,
Phoenix 6.137 s, against maxima of 10.0 s, 12.8 s and 17.9 s. The tails are the story.

Any future overhead claim in this cluster needs either far more runs or a workload whose
wall time is not dominated by a network round trip to a model provider.

## Cost and volume

- **$0.099** total model spend, 540 provider requests, `gpt-4o`, temperature 0.
- 540 not 600: each run issues 10 LLM steps but the `llm-error` scenario's doomed call raises
  before the model is reached. 9 real calls × 60 runs = 540. This matches the offline test
  finding that only 9 of 10 LLM spans can carry token data, and is a useful internal
  consistency check on the whole pipeline.
- Server: roughly €0.06 for the session.

## The findings that actually matter: two near-miss false accusations

Both would have been publishable-looking, severe, and wrong. Neither is a Langfuse defect.

**1. `fields=metadata` and the default projection are mutually exclusive.**
`GET /api/public/v2/observations` returns `name`, `level` and `statusMessage` as `null` when
metadata is requested, and drops metadata when they are. Our correlation key lives in
metadata, so the first reader saw `level=None` on every record and scored **every injected
error as uncaptured**. The first smoke run duly reported "Langfuse 0/2 errors, Phoenix 2/2" —
a clean, plausible, completely false headline. Corrected by joining two calls on observation
id.

**2. The endpoint is cursor-paginated and silently ignores `page`.**
Requesting pages 2–5 returns the identical first 100 rows with an unchanged `meta.cursor`. A
page-number reader collects exactly 100 unique records regardless of how much data exists.
Against 400 issued spans that reads as **25% capture**. A single `limit=500` call returned all
402 records, proving nothing had been dropped.

Both bugs **pass a two-span positive control** and only appear at volume. That is the
methodological lesson worth publishing: a positive control that asks "did anything arrive"
is necessary and not sufficient. The gate should assert a known *count*, not a known span.

## Threats to validity, recorded

- **Phoenix's store was not cleanly wiped.** The pre-run `pkill` did not stop the old server
  process, so 28 spans from the earlier smoke session and probes survived into the scored
  store (428 records returned, 400 belonging to the scored run). Capture is correlated on
  `(run_id, step_index)`, so the scored result is unaffected — but the store was not pristine
  and saying so is cheaper than being asked.
- **Manual instrumentation in both arms**, auto-instrumentation disabled. Deviation from the
  frozen protocol, recorded in the protocol itself with reasoning. The study therefore says
  nothing about either project's default auto-instrumentation — which is what most readers
  actually install. Strongest candidate for the follow-up.
- Scripted control flow: measures capture of a known agent-shaped workload, not of an
  arbitrary framework integration.
- One model, one provider, one host, self-hosted only, 20 spans per trace. Long-horizon
  traces with hundreds of spans are untested and batching may differ materially at scale.
- Datadog, LangSmith, Comet Opik, Helicone and Braintrust are not measured.

## Editorial consequence

There is no winner to declare, so the article cannot be a versus piece. What it has instead
is the thing no page on this SERP has: an actual measurement with a denominator we own, a
published null result, and two concrete, reproducible API traps that would have made a
careless benchmark lie. That is the honest angle and it is a stronger one than a fake ranking.
